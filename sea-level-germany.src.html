What a reef can and cannot do

It does not slow sea level rise

The mean level of the North Sea and Baltic rises with ocean warming and melting ice. No structure on the coast changes that. A breakwater or reef works on the waves on top of the water: it breaks them, so less water runs up and over the dike during a storm.

Living reefs can grow with the sea

A rock breakwater stays where it was built, so each centimetre of sea level rise means deeper water over its crest and bigger waves passing through. Oyster reefs and salt marshes trap sediment and build upward. As long as the sea rises slower than they grow, they keep their effect.

No coral in German waters

Reef-building corals need water above about 18 °C all year. The North Sea and Baltic are far too cold. The coral option is a reference case from tropical coasts. The realistic nature-based options here are intertidal oyster reefs (North Sea only, the Baltic is not salty enough), mussel beds, seagrass and salt marsh forelands.
